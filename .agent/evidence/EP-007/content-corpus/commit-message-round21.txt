content: what a pack teaches, in the product (REQ-003, REQ-023, REQ-032, REQ-048, REQ-056)

Round 20 gave the pack format the curriculum graph and the calibration metadata the spec lists,
and recorded honestly that neither was visible: they travelled in the pack, the installer
verified them, and no learner or reviewer could see them. This round closes that, and finds a
defect on the way.

## What it adds

**The content manager shows what a pack teaches.** `InstalledPackDto` gains one entry per
objective the pack's manifest declares -- its title, subtest, prerequisites and calibration --
and the packs panel renders them under the pack:

```
What core-asvab v3 teaches
  Auto Information: what a component is for (AI) -- after OBJ-SI-TOOLS-01
      -- 53% expected correct, declared, no responses recorded
```

The sentence turns on `responses`. A difficulty figure with no responses behind it is a
declaration made from the material, not an observation of learners, and the panel says
"declared, no responses recorded" rather than printing a percentage that reads like a
measurement. A pack whose manifest predates the curriculum says so, instead of rendering an
empty list as if it were one.

**The plan follows the curriculum.** `Services::study_plan` reads the active pack's graph and
moves a drill that teaches a prerequisite ahead of the drill that requires it, appending to the
drill's own reason: `... ; it is the prerequisite for AI in this pack's curriculum`. Two things
make that honest rather than clever:

* the order is the *pack's*, not this layer's opinion of the subject. A device with no pack
  installed has no declared curriculum, and its plan is unchanged;
* the move is conservative. It pulls a prerequisite earlier only when both subtests are already
  planned, never pushes a dependent drill past anything else, and never replaces the planner's
  own reason -- a learner with fifteen minutes should not have their session stretched to reach
  something they will meet tomorrow, and the planner's judgement about why and how long is
  still true after the reordering.

## The defect the work found

`objectives_from_manifest` first parsed the registry's stored manifest as a `PackDocument`. The
installer stores the pack's *payload* -- the bytes the signature covers, without the signature
itself -- so every pack listed zero objectives and the panel would have shown "this pack
declares no curriculum" for a pack that declares six. The integration test caught it on its
first run (`left: 0, right: 4`), which is what the test was for: the field would otherwise have
been populated by nothing and rendered as a blank list nobody could distinguish from an old
pack.

## Verification

The behaviour is covered at three levels, because each can fail on its own: a Rust test that an
installed pack reports the graph and the calibration it was built with, a view test that the
panel renders the prerequisite edge and distinguishes a declared figure from a measured one, and
an end-to-end test in the built bundle through the stub boundary.

`mutation-round18.py` now proves 26 rules, including both of this round's: removing the
prerequisite ordering fails the planner test, and emptying `objectives_from_manifest` fails the
DTO test.

Corpus unchanged at 5,621 active items (1,949 WK, 1,565 EI, 1,676 PC, 301 GS, 55 SI, 75 AI),
69 evidence records, 7,570 citations, pack `core-asvab v3` active, every provenance invariant
zero. `sh scripts/verify.sh` exits 0 with all 19 gates recorded exit 0: 1,717 Rust tests across
109 binaries, 211 frontend unit tests, 36 E2E, packaged live-fire passed.

## Still open

SI 55 and AI 75 remain thin for a subtest that asks sixteen questions in a sitting. The plan
orders by prerequisite but does not yet track mastery *per objective*: attempts are recorded per
item and items carry an objective id, so per-objective mastery is derivable, and the plan still
reasons about subtests while the curriculum's finer grain is used only for ordering. General
