content: read the manuals' other half, and ask each question once (REQ-022, REQ-023, REQ-056)

Auto Information exists, the reader knows four more ways a manual states what a thing is
for, and the corpus turns out to be smaller than it was reported -- because 3,305 of its
items were questions it had already asked.

## What it adds

`crates/vector-questions/src/purposes.rs` reads the other side of a description. Round 17
formed "Which tool is used to remove broken screws?" from `Screw extractors are used to
remove broken screws`; the same sentence now also forms "What are the screw extractors used
for?", with the functions of the manual's other components as the wrong answers. Two Army
manuals supply the Auto Information bank: TM 9-8000 (1985) and TM 9-2700, both Internet
Archive scans recorded in the vault with their own URL and digest.

The relation is stated in more than one set of words, and each shape the reader could not
see was content already in the corpus: `scripts/probes/description-shapes.py` counted them
before any rule was written -- in TM 9-8000 alone, `is designed to` 66 times and `serves to`
23 times beside 134 sentences written `is used to`. Reading `is designed to`, `is intended
to` and `serves to` took the two automotive manuals from 46 descriptions to 78. `serves as`
is deliberately not read: its complement is a noun, and these frames take a purpose clause.

## The rules, and the defects they were written for

Every rule below was written against a sentence that reached a learner, and every one is
mutation-proved: `scripts/probes/mutation-round18.py` disables one rule at a time in the
source and requires the test that should catch it to fail. All 16 mutations are caught. A
rule enforced in two places is disabled in both.

* **A purpose opens with a verb these manuals use.** The previous rule refused only what was
  obviously not a verb, which accepted `used for heavy, for medium, and for light duty only`
  and built "Which tool is used to heavy, ...?" -- an item with no answer. The list is not
  guessed: `scripts/probes/purpose-verbs.py` prints every distinct word a purpose opens with
  across the sources, and the 205 entries are that output. The price is measured: 115
  descriptions under the permissive rule, 103 under the list.
* **Punctuation is not part of the word it follows.** `used for gripping, reaching places`
  reached the corpus as `used to gripping`.
* **A purpose is a clause and holds no full stop** -- `cut this fuzz from the wood.
  "Sandpaper" consists of small particles of flint` was one answer, because the opening quote
  after the stop kept the sentence splitter from ending the sentence.
* **Latin letters only.** An archived TM 9-8000 offers `Oй filters`, `Tһe bulkhead
  receptacle` and `fluid pressure юг the lubrication system`; the letters that are
  shape-identical to Latin ones are normalised, and the rest are refused rather than guessed.
* **A class is not a tool.** `the more permanent type`, `Whatever method`, `Both hands` and
  `catch trough then` all describe something; none of them is a thing a shop stocks.
* **A possessive names a document**, not an object: `automotive manufacturers' requirements`.
* **Scan damage is refused, not repaired**: `be- tween`, `EVAPORATOR CORE CAPILLARY TUBE`,
  `of to candlepower`, `at least gallons liters) per minute` -- a broken word, a drawing's
  label read into a sentence, and a number the scan dropped.
* **A name is not ended by its own first word**: `Inside micrometers` and `Outside calipers`
  are tools, and the preposition list was truncating them to nothing.
* **An Auto Information option is an infinitive.** The manuals state functions both ways, and
  framing a gerund produced `to joining the towing vehicle and the trailer with safety
  chains`. The manual's words are not conjugated into a form it did not write: those
  descriptions are asked as Shop Information questions instead.
* **A lost space is refused**: `damagingthe`, `ina vise`, `riveted,or`, and `engine-todrive
  train clearance`.

## One question, asked once

`ContentItemRepo::question_exists` identifies a question by what a learner answers -- the
stem, the correct answer, and the passage if there is one -- and all five ingest paths ask it
before storing. The identity is deliberately that coarse and no coarser: `Choose the word
that most nearly means the same as irenic` has more than one true answer, and the 59 Word
Knowledge stems with a different correct answer are 59 questions, not duplicates.

Two things were hiding behind the missing check.

**A refused item left a draft behind.** Storing is a sequence -- insert a draft, cite the
source, record the verifier's hash, walk it to content-reviewed, activate it -- and the row
exists from the first step, so a failure in the middle left it in the store for good. An
ingestion naming a source the vault had not recorded produced six drafts, and because a draft
is a row the new check read them as questions already asked: the run reported six items
already present and activated none while its own rejection list showed every item refused.
Fixed with `ContentItemRepo::discard_draft` (guarded to `state = 'draft'`) and
`ContentPipeline::finish_item`, which wraps the tail of all six store paths.

**The Electronics Information bank was 89% the same question.** It was reported as 3,668
items; ingesting it again produced 3,668 items of which 3,259 were already asked -- the same
NEETS definition, the same correct term, the same four options in a different order. The bank
is 414 questions, which is the whole of what those glossaries state in a shape an item can be
built from: asked for 3,000 items per module the builder produces about 10,000 candidates and
414 questions, and a rebuild from an empty store reproduces all 414.

**A citation could not be re-checked.** Ten Paragraph Comprehension items cited
`https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2009` for the text of `darwin-origin.txt`, which is Project
Gutenberg #1228. The provenance check passed because it re-downloads the works named in the
manifest, and the manifest had been corrected while the vault record and the items citing it
had not. The stale record is gone and the work is re-ingested from the manifest.

## Tooling

`scripts/rebuild-corpus.py` rebuilds every subtest from its sources in one run, prunes the
vault record that is not evidence, and writes the round's reports; `--dry-run` reports first.
`scripts/probes/fetch-archive-text.py` downloads an Internet Archive `_djvu.txt`;
`purpose-verbs.py`, `description-shapes.py`, `foreign-letters.py` and `mutation-round18.py`
measure what the rules were written against. `check-corpus.py` gained four invariants,
including an independent Python implementation of the scan-damage test -- which first
reported 3,681 damaged items, 3,670 of them `THERMOCOUPLE`-style Electronics Information
options that are the NEETS glossary's own typography. The capitals rule is scoped to the
subtests a program framed from a manual's sentence.

Seven Internet Archive NAVEDTRA manuals were downloaded, measured and refused
(*Construction Mechanic 3 & 2*, *Aviation Machinist's Mate R 1 & C*, *Fluid Power*, *Aviation
Structural Mechanic E 3 & 2*, *Machinery Repairman 3 & 2*, *Patternmaker 3 and 2*,
*Construction Electrician 3 & 2*): their microfiche scans write `reiatively`, `chu-k` and
`ordir.aty`, their sentences run into one another, and what survives the rules is a name that
names nothing (`Special equipment`, `Relatively large amounts`, `Single-acting cylinders
similar`).
