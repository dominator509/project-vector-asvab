## What it adds

**Mastery per objective.** `AttemptRepo::objective_stats` joins each attempt through the item it
was on to the objective that item teaches: an attempt records the question, the question records
the objective, so this is read rather than maintained. `Services::objective_mastery` reports, per
objective the installed pack declares, the attempts, the correct count, the same Laplace estimate
the subtest mastery uses (`(correct + 1) / (attempts + 2)`, so no evidence is 0.5 rather than 0),
and `waiting_on` -- the prerequisites this learner has not met.

**A threshold, in one place.** `PREREQUISITE_MET = 0.6` against that estimate: no attempts gives
0.5, one correct out of one gives 0.67, one wrong out of one gives 0.33. An untouched prerequisite
is therefore *unmet* rather than assumed known, one correct answer is enough to pass it, and one
wrong answer is not. The number sits in a named constant with that reasoning attached, because it
is a judgement and a reader should be able to disagree with it.

**The plan names the objective.** Each drill carries the objective to work on -- the weakest whose
prerequisites are met, ties broken by fewer attempts and then by id so two plans from the same
evidence agree -- and its reason says which and why. An objective whose prerequisite is unmet is
not offered at all: the curriculum says it comes later, and offering it now is what the graph
exists to prevent. Objectives, subtests and prerequisites all come from the installed manifest; a
device with no pack names no objective.

## The defect the live readback found

The service tests prove behaviour against a fixture. `crates/vector-application/examples/
plan_report.rs` opens a *copy* of the installation's own database -- the pack it installed, the
corpus ingested into it -- creates a learner and prints the plan. Its first run included:

```
  AO     4 min  objective -   weakness
```

AO is Assembling Objects, and this installation has neither items nor templates for it. The
subtest exists in the domain, the planner ranks every subtest in the domain, and the plan sent the
learner to a drill that could not start. Unit tests, view tests and E2E all passed; it was visible
only by reading what a real store produces.

Only subtests this installation can serve are now handed to the planner (templates to generate
from, or items already stored). The filter is in the service rather than on the planner's output,
because filtering afterwards would leave the freed minutes allocated to a drill that is not in the
plan and the interface checks that a plan's total equals its drills.

Two service tests then failed, and that was the filter working: they wanted a plan with SI and AI
in it while their fixture store held nothing but factory-generated Arithmetic Reasoning items --
the same assumption, in miniature, that the readback caught in the product. The fixture now ingests
a Shop and an Auto Information manual through the real pipeline, dictionary check and all.
